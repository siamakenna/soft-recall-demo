# Research Basis

Soft Recall Demo uses an optional research layer to document ideas that informed a few mechanics. Main narration remains fictional and citation-free.

## Research-Informed Mechanics

- Confidence choices and reflection draw on work about metamemory and self-monitoring.
- The phone draft draws on prospective memory: remembering an intention for later.
- Notes, reminders, and consistent object placement draw on cue use and external memory support.
- Hallway landmarks and Doorways draw on research about spatial navigation and orientation.
- The Memory Book uses the broad idea of recording observations outside one's immediate recall.

These connections are design context. The game does not reproduce a research task, measure cognition, or represent one specific health condition.

## In-Game Sources

Only entries marked both `verified` and `visibleInGame` in [`src/data/researchLinks.ts`](../../src/data/researchLinks.ts) appear under Memory Book -> Learn More. The list currently includes an official National Institute on Aging overview and PubMed-indexed work on metamemory, prospective memory, spatial navigation, and memory supports.

Plain-language relevance appears before each link. Every entry also states what the game cannot conclude from that source.

## Claim Boundaries

Soft Recall Demo does not claim to diagnose, treat, prevent, or screen for any disease or condition. A fictional interaction cannot establish why someone forgets, feels uncertain, needs a cue, or becomes overwhelmed. Similar experiences can have many contexts and meanings.

Soft Recall Demo is narrative and research-informed. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, screening, treatment, or a clinical assessment tool.
